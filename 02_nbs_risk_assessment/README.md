# 02. Nature-Based Solutions (NbS) Risk Assessment

## Project Overview

This assessment developed and applied a structured risk framework for evaluating the integrity and procurement suitability of nature-based carbon projects.

The analysis combined carbon-credit integrity assessment, registry research, project-documentation review and geospatial evidence to identify risks that could affect carbon-removal credibility, environmental outcomes and procurement decisions.

## Assessment Objectives

1. Develop a weighted risk framework covering the principal risks associated with nature-based carbon removals.
2. Identify open-source datasets, remote-sensing products, registries and public information sources for evaluating project performance and environmental integrity.
3. Apply the framework to two Verra-registered projects:
   - African Carbon Partnership – Gashaka Gumti, Nigeria.
   - Fidelis – Trees for Farmers, India.
4. Identify material concerns, evidence gaps and additional verification requirements relevant to procurement decisions.

## Assessment Components

- **Risk framework:** Risk categories, proposed weights, assessment criteria and procurement relevance.
- **Data and evidence:** Registry records, project documentation, satellite observations and geospatial datasets.
- **Project assessments:** Review of the two selected projects, including reported carbon estimates, additionality, permanence, leakage and safeguards.
- **Decision implications:** Identification of purchase-critical issues and evidence required before contracting.

## Analytical Approach

The assessment used publicly available project information and geospatial evidence to examine the consistency, credibility and limitations of reported project claims.

Registry information was cross-checked against project descriptions and monitoring documentation. Geospatial datasets were considered as complementary evidence for evaluating land-cover change, forest condition, fire exposure and potential leakage.

## Scope and Limitations

This work represents a technical assessment based on publicly available information at the time of review. It is not an independent validation, verification or certification of either project, and identified concerns should be interpreted as issues requiring further investigation rather than definitive evidence of project failure.
